# Anti-patterns

These patterns produce the appearance of scholarship while weakening its evidentiary basis.

## Prompt-to-paper

**Pattern:** Ask for a complete paper from a broad topic.  
**Risk:** Prose precedes the research decisions needed to justify it.  
**Use instead:** `formulating-research-question` → `scoping-argument-boundary`.

## Citation decoration

**Pattern:** Add references after drafting to make claims look supported.  
**Risk:** Real or invented sources may not support the sentence.  
**Use instead:** `auditing-citations`.

## Theory-name accumulation

**Pattern:** List theorists without mapping inheritance, disagreement, or modification.  
**Risk:** Name density substitutes for conceptual work.  
**Use instead:** `mapping-concept-lineage`.

## Summary-list literature review

**Pattern:** One paragraph per scholar with no relation among positions.  
**Risk:** The review reports but does not argue.  
**Use instead:** `conducting-literature-dialogue`.

## Stylistic synonym drift

**Pattern:** Replace a core term with near-synonyms to avoid repetition.  
**Risk:** The concept changes unnoticed.  
**Use instead:** `checking-terminology-consistency`.

## Quote-first interpretation

**Pattern:** Collect quotations and assume their relevance is self-evident.  
**Risk:** Evidence is not connected to a warranted interpretation.  
**Use instead:** `performing-close-reading`.

## Reviewer theater

**Pattern:** Ask an AI reviewer for praise or generic weaknesses.  
**Risk:** Review becomes performance rather than stress testing.  
**Use instead:** `reviewing-manuscript` with severity, evidence, and required action.

## Completion by tone

**Pattern:** Accept “publication-ready” because the prose is polished.  
**Risk:** Unresolved citations, placeholders, and argument gaps remain hidden.  
**Use instead:** `verifying-before-submission`.
