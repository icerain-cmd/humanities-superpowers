# Design principles

## 1. Research before prose

Do not default to paragraph generation. Produce research records when the task requires decisions, evidence, or verification.

## 2. Unknown is better than fabricated

Use explicit markers such as `UNKNOWN`, `UNVERIFIED`, and `SOURCE REQUIRED`.

## 3. Evidence over confidence

A claim is not strengthened by assertive tone. Record the evidence and the warrant.

## 4. Bounded claims over inflated novelty

Novelty claims must match the scope of the literature search actually performed.

## 5. Concepts require lineage and boundaries

Define inherited meanings, modifications, exclusions, and translations.

## 6. Argument before style

Do not polish unsupported prose. Repair claims, reasons, warrants, and evidence first.

## 7. Strong objections before submission

Stress-test the argument against serious alternatives, not weak caricatures.

## 8. Citations require two checks

Verify both existence and claim–source fit.

## 9. Completion must be demonstrated

“Finished” is a claim that requires a gate report.

## 10. The smallest sufficient workflow

Do not force every task through every skill. Use only the methods needed for the current research state.

## 11. Researcher decisions remain visible

Mark choices that require disciplinary, ethical, or interpretive judgment.

## 12. Validation can fail

A framework that cannot refuse completion is not a quality framework.
