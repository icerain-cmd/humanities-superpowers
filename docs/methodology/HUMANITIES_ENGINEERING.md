# Humanities Engineering

Humanities Engineering is the practice of translating humanities principles of interpretation, evidence, judgment, disagreement, and revision into explicit computational protocols without reducing them to automated decisions.

It does not reduce humanities inquiry to quantitative scoring, grant an AI the status of a scholar, or automate human judgment. It encodes methodological constraints so software must preserve uncertainty, provenance, rival readings, counterevidence, revision history, and the researcher's authority.

In Humanities Superpowers 2.0, this practice appears as five explicit objects: evidence-ledger entries, judgment records, interpretation versions, friction events, and epistemic-return records. They do not decide whether an interpretation is true. They make it harder for fluent output to conceal what remains contested and who authorized a scholarly choice.

The method remains portable Markdown, JSON Schema, synthetic fixtures, and deterministic validation. Memory may preserve workflow context; memory is not evidence.
