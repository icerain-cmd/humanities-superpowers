# Scope boundary

| dimension | included | excluded | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| corpus | three public-facing interfaces | all platforms | permits close comparison |
| period | 2024–2026 versions | platform history as a whole | controls technical change |
| analytical level | interface framing and user action possibilities | infrastructure lifecycle and global political economy | different evidence would be required |
| claim | mediation reorganizes perceptual and practical access | mediation determines all environmental belief | avoids causal overclaim |
| concept | artificial nature as relational effect | artificial nature as a new ontological substance | keeps concept analytically bounded |

## Scope statement

This study examines three public-facing platform interfaces available between 2024 and 2026. It analyzes how their visual, navigational, and classificatory operations frame environmental phenomena as observable, navigable, or governable. “Artificial nature” is used as a limited analytical term for a relational effect produced through interface mediation, not as a claim that platforms replace material environments or determine users’ beliefs. Infrastructure, energy use, labor, and platform political economy remain relevant but fall outside the article’s primary evidentiary scope.
